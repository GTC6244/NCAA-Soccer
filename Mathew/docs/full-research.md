# How to use this document

This is recruiting research for men's college soccer, in three tiers.

**The Core 12** are the primary targets — the schools from the original list that actually sponsor varsity men's soccer.

**The Extras** are additional Florida-to-North-Carolina schools added to widen the net, ranked by enrollment. Secondary for now: contacts included where available, but not researched to the same depth.

**The Canadian schools** sit outside the NCAA entirely and shouldn't be compared directly against the US programs.

## Standing caveats

- Enrollment figures are approximate. They come from general knowledge rather than each school's official fact book. Rankings separated by 1,000–2,000 students could flip on a current IPEDS pull.
- Coaching staffs change constantly. Several schools here hired new head coaches in 2025–26. Verify before sending anything.
- Many programs publish no individual coach emails. Where that's true it's stated. A shared inbox or the questionnaire is the intended channel, not a workaround.
- Two different GPA bars apply. NCAA initial eligibility is a 2.3 core-course GPA on a sliding scale with test scores. University admission is a separate and usually much higher bar.

# Core 12 — academics

## The exception

**UC Santa Barbara has no undergraduate business administration major.** It offers Economics & Accounting plus a Technology Management program (certificate/minor). If a business degree is a hard requirement, UCSB is a problem despite being strong everywhere else. Worth confirming directly with their admissions office.

Computer science is a restricted major at Penn State, Ohio State, UC Santa Barbara and NJIT — admission to the university does not guarantee admission to the major. Plan for a separate application and a higher GPA threshold at those four.

# Canadian universities (U SPORTS)

These six sit outside the NCAA entirely and the rules are different enough that they shouldn't be compared directly against the US schools.

## How U SPORTS differs

- **Governing body:** U SPORTS, not the NCAA. All six play in Ontario University Athletics (OUA). There is no NCAA Eligibility Center registration and the 2.3 core-course GPA rule does not apply.
- **Money works differently.** No athletic scholarships in the US sense. Athletic Financial Awards are capped at tuition plus compulsory fees — no room, board or living costs. An entrance award in Ontario requires an 80% high school average.
- **Short season.** Roughly 16 regular-season games from late August through October, then OUA playoffs and the U SPORTS national championship in November.
- **Cost.** Domestic tuition is a fraction of US private or out-of-state rates, which changes the financial calculus even without scholarship money.
- **Eligibility is five years** of competition, and transferring between U SPORTS and NCAA has its own rules worth checking early.

## Program form

- **Toronto** won the 2025 OUA championship, their 51st, beating York 2-0 in the final at Varsity Stadium. They led the OUA with 42 goals and conceded a province-low eight. They lose nine starters, so 2026 is a rebuild with openings.
- **York** were 2025 OUA finalists and their roster pulls from Toronto FC II, York United and League1 Ontario — a strong pro-pathway environment.
- **Western** took OUA bronze in 2018 under Painter.
- **Waterloo** reached the 2025 OUA semifinal (lost 2-0 to No. 3 Toronto) — a strong rebound and a second-place OUA West finish after a lean 2024, in Jon Morgan's second year as head coach.
- **Guelph** went 5-3-4 in 2024 and won an opening-round OUA playoff on penalties over Laurier. New head coach Alan Koch, a former MLS head coach at FC Cincinnati, took over in January 2025.
- **Laurier** were national champions in 2000 and 2001 and OUA champions in 2000 and 2008, but recent seasons have been mid-table in a deep OUA West.

## Academics

All six offer math, physics, computer science and business. Two caveats on the business side: Western's Ivey Business School is an Advanced Entry Opportunity — you apply to a different faculty first and enter Ivey in third year. York's Schulich is direct-entry but separately competitive. U of T's Rotman Commerce is also a separate, competitive stream. None of those three is a straightforward "admitted to the university, therefore admitted to business" path.

The three additions each have a distinct academic identity. **Waterloo** is the strongest computer-science and engineering school of the group — home to the first Faculty of Mathematics in North America (1967) and the world's largest co-operative education program, and one of Google's top recruiting universities globally. **Guelph** is No. 1 in Canada for veterinary science (6th in the world, QS 2025) through the Ontario Veterinary College, and strong across agriculture and life sciences. **Laurier's** draw is the Lazaridis School of Business & Economics plus a top-ranked Faculty of Music.

# School profiles

Deep-dive profiles covering the soccer program, academics, geography and institutional character of each school. Built in batches. Schools not yet profiled are listed at the end.

A standing note on **formations**: college programs don't publish tactical shape and match reports rarely describe it. Each profile gives a results-grounded read on how the team actually plays where that's supportable, and says nothing where it isn't.

A standing note on **drive times to the Canadian border**: this only discriminates meaningfully for the Northeast and Midwest. Anything in Florida, Texas or California is 20+ hours and should be treated as fly-only.

## SMU — Dallas, Texas

### Soccer program

Founded 1975; 2026 is season 51. **Kevin Hudson** is only the fourth head coach in program history — named January 2015, but on the Hilltop 19 years. Head-coaching record 115-44-23 (.695). The program holds 37 NCAA tournament appearances, two College Cups (2000, 2005) and Elite Eight runs in 2010, 2019 and 2024.

2025 was the breakthrough: SMU won its **first-ever ACC tournament title**, with Slade Starnes taking tournament MVP. They enter 2026 defending an ACC title for the first time in program history, opened 5-0-1 and won 3-1 at Pitt to start conference play, ranked No. 7. The 2023 side produced four All-Americans — the most of any Division I program that year.

Home is **Washburne Soccer and Track Stadium**, capacity 2,577 — a dedicated soccer venue. Ten of 15 matches at home in 2026.

**Staff:** Kevin Hudson (HC), Michael King (Assoc HC, recruiting coordinator), Jaime Ibarra-Perez (Assoc HC, goalkeeping), Ross Fitzpatrick (Assistant).

**Mascot:** Peruna, a live black Shetland pony who gallops the sideline after goals.

### Academics

Private, founded 1911. 7,285 undergraduates of ~12,000 total. US News #88 nationally for 2026. Student-faculty ratio **11:1**, **average class size 22**, 70% of classes at 25 or fewer, 54% under 20. Freshman retention 91%. Tuition and fees $69,722.

**Business — Cox School.** Direct-admit, and you must name a business major as your primary academic interest on the application. Acceptance rate 35.7%, average SAT 1,435, average high school GPA 3.77. Poets&Quants ranked Cox **#25 nationally for undergraduate business** in 2026. Average class size in Cox is 35–40.

**CS, math, physics — no secondary admission.** CS sits in the Bobby B. Lyle School of Engineering (~1,000 undergrads, 60+ faculty, ABET accredited), which has no admission requirements beyond university admission. Penn State, Ohio State, UCSB and NJIT all gate CS behind a second competitive application.

### Geography

**Not a college town.** The 234-acre campus sits in University Park, an affluent independent municipality surrounded by Dallas, five miles north of downtown. Compact and walkable, Georgian Revival red brick centred on Dallas Hall. DART light rail from Mockingbird Station runs straight downtown.

**Airports:** Dallas Love Field ~15 minutes (Southwest hub); DFW International ~30–35 minutes.

**Canadian border:** ~1,450 miles, ~21–22 hours. Fly-only.

**Housing:** Residential Commons system with a two-year on-campus requirement. Confirm current policy.

### Known for

The George W. Bush Presidential Center sits on campus. "The Boulevard" is the tailgating tradition along Bishop Boulevard. SMU is the only school ever given the NCAA "death penalty" — football shut down entirely in 1987. Alumni include Laura Bush, Kathy Bates, Aaron Spelling, Lauren Graham, Doak Walker, Eric Dickerson and Don Meredith.

## UCF — Orlando, Florida

## Penn State — University Park, Pennsylvania

### Soccer program

**The head coach is Canadian.** Rob Dow was born in Cole Harbour, Nova Scotia. He played at Maine, has a master's from North Dakota State, and spent nine seasons as head coach at Vermont. Worth knowing before any outreach — a Nova Scotian running a Big Ten program is likely to read a Canadian recruit's background with more context than most.

Public land-grant, founded 1855. **42,619 undergraduates** at University Park. Campus **7,958 acres** — by far the largest on either list. US News **#59 nationally, #26 among publics**. Student-faculty ratio **15–16:1**. Retention 93%, four-year graduation 71%, six-year 86%.

A statistic that matters more than the ratio: **89% of faculty are full-time, against a national average of 47%.** You are taught by career academics rather than adjuncts. That said, introductory courses run in lecture halls of around 300, with small-group work in recitations and labs.

In-state $21,098; out-of-state $43,490.

The flip side is real: no major city nearby, long cold winters, and students without a car move on foot and by bus.

### Known for

One of the largest alumni networks in the world. Football culture dominates autumn; Beaver Stadium holds over 106,000. THON, the student-run dance marathon, is the largest student philanthropy in the world. Strong in engineering, supply chain, meteorology and geosciences.

## UC Santa Barbara — Goleta, California

### Soccer program

### Academics

Public, founded 1909. **23,181 undergraduates**, 1,055-acre campus. US News **#40 nationally and #14 among publics**. Student-faculty ratio 17:1 with **56.5% of classes under 20 students**. 90% of faculty full-time. Retention 92% and an **85% four-year graduation rate — the highest of any school profiled so far.**

**Two structural things to know first.**

**UCSB runs on a quarter system**, not semesters — three ten-week terms instead of two fifteen-week ones. Faster pace, more finals, a different rhythm alongside an autumn sports season.

**The UC system is test-blind.** SAT and ACT scores are not considered at all, even if submitted. Admission rests on grades, coursework rigour and essays.

**Admission:** roughly 28–38% overall depending on cycle. Unusually, **the out-of-state rate has run higher than the in-state rate** — around 51.5% versus 32% in the most recent complete data — because the UCs reserve most places for Californians and treat non-residents as a separate pool. Mathematics, physics, economics and biology are individually selective with lower rates than the university overall.

Cost of attendance runs roughly $45,000 for California residents and **around $80,000 for non-residents**, where international students sit. The most expensive public option on the Core 12.

**Physics is world class.** UCSB hosts the **Kavli Institute for Theoretical Physics**, where six Nobel laureates have worked. Materials science and chemical engineering are top-20 nationally and widely regarded as the strongest in the UC system.

**Computer science** ranks **#35 undergraduate and #27 for graduate programs**, with 42 full-time faculty. Mathematics is among the most popular majors.

**The business gap is real and confirmed.** There is no undergraduate business administration major — only Economics & Accounting and the Technology Management program. **If a business degree is a requirement, UCSB does not meet it.** The one hard disqualifier among the Core 12.

**The College of Creative Studies** is a distinctive asset: a small, honours-style college where students design highly independent curricula in mathematics, physics and computing.

### Geography

**The most unusual campus setting on either list — it is literally on the beach.** UCSB sits on a bluff above the Pacific with ocean on two sides and a lagoon through the middle, in Goleta, about ten miles from downtown Santa Barbara.

**Isla Vista** is the adjacent unincorporated student town where most upperclassmen live — one of the densest residential communities in the United States. Convenient, walkable and entirely student; it also carries a long-standing party reputation worth discussing honestly rather than discovering later.

**Airports:** Santa Barbara Municipal is ten minutes away but small. LAX is roughly two hours by car with frequent direct Toronto service. **A weak local link offset by a strong one two hours away.**

**Canadian border:** roughly 1,200 miles to Blaine, Washington — about **18–19 hours**. Fly-only, and the longest flight of the Core 12 from Ontario.

**Nearby:** downtown Santa Barbara 10 miles, Los Angeles 1.5–2 hours, Santa Ynez wine country 45 minutes.

**Housing:** the university guarantees limited years on campus and the surrounding market is severely constrained and expensive — a well-documented issue at UCSB specifically. Ask the coaching staff how the team handles it.

### Known for

World-leading physics and materials science research. A campus consistently ranked among the most beautiful in the country. Strong environmental science through the Bren School. Michael Douglas is an alumnus. For soccer, UCSB is the only program on either list with a national championship and an attendance record nobody else has touched.

Nationally recognised for dramatically raising graduation rates and closing achievement gaps — Georgia State's student-success work has been studied and copied across American higher education. Majors include accounting, economics, entrepreneurship, finance, hospitality management, information systems, management, marketing and professional sales, supported by a trading floor, a 3D printing lab and an information security lab.

The College of Engineering and Engineering Technology is the other substantial unit, alongside the Wellstar School of Nursing. Computer and information sciences are offered. Maths and physics exist but are not institutional strengths the way they are at UCSB or NJIT.

### Geography

**Suburban Atlanta, about 25 to 30 miles northwest of the city** in Cobb County. Green campus with a lake and a nature preserve — a different feel from Georgia State's downtown blocks an hour south.

**Formerly a pure commuter school, KSU now houses more than 5,000 students** — meaningful growth, but still a small share of nearly 43,000 undergraduates.

**Airports:** Hartsfield-Jackson is the world's busiest, but sits on the far south side of the metro — **45 to 60 minutes from campus in traffic**, not the 20 minutes Georgia State enjoys. Excellent routing including direct Toronto service, just a longer trip to reach it.

**Canadian border:** roughly 900 miles to Detroit–Windsor, about **13 to 14 hours**.

**Nearby:** downtown Atlanta 30–40 minutes, Kennesaw Mountain National Battlefield adjacent, the north Georgia mountains about an hour.

**Housing:** on-campus capacity above 5,000 and growing; Cobb County's rental market is moderate and considerably cheaper than intown Atlanta.

### Known for

Rapid growth from a junior college to one of Georgia's largest universities. The Coles College of Business. A recently ascendant athletics department. Strong regional employer connections in the Atlanta metro, though the national profile remains modest.

**Bottom line:** the cheap out-of-state tuition and confirmed Division I program make this worth a phone call, but the 24% four-year graduation rate and the total absence of publicly available soccer information mean it should sit low on the list until you have spoken to someone there directly.

## UNC Chapel Hill — Chapel Hill, North Carolina

### Soccer program

**Two national championships — 2001 and 2011 — plus a runner-up finish in 2008 and nine College Cup appearances.** Thirty-two NCAA tournament appearances. Founded 1947, and in 79 seasons **the program has had only two losing years.** That consistency is unmatched anywhere in this research.

**Carlos Somoano enters his 16th season** with a career record of **185-68-55**, including 67-31-28 in ACC play. His 185 wins rank second all-time at Carolina and his 67 ACC wins are the most of any head coach in program history.

**The 2011 season is one of the great stories in college soccer.** Somoano won the national championship **in his first year as a head coach** — only the second rookie head coach in NCAA history to do so — going 21-2-3 and taking the ACC regular season, the ACC Championship and the national title in the same year. Only the second team in ACC history to claim that treble. He was Soccer America's National Coach of the Year and ACC Coach of the Year, joining Anson Dorrance as the only coaches named ACC Coach of the Year in their first season.

**The development record is the most relevant thing for a recruit.** Somoano has coached **two National Players of the Year, 19 All-Americans, four ACC Players of the Year, three ACC Rookies of the Year, and 36 MLS SuperDraft selections or homegrown signings.** He has taken 14 of his 15 squads to the NCAA tournament, seven to at least the quarterfinals, with 22 tournament wins.

**Stability is secured:** Somoano signed a three-year contract extension in April 2026.

**2026:** 15 regular-season games, nine at home. Three opponents finished 2025 in the top 25 and six made the NCAA tournament. They opened at Georgetown and Memphis before hosting VCU. Carolina finished 2025 receiving votes in the final coaches poll, then went 5-0 in the spring to win the Carolina Cup. The regular season closes at home against NC State.

Home is **Dorrance Field at Play2Dream Legacy Stadium**, capacity 4,200 — named for Anson Dorrance.

**Staff:** Carlos Somoano (HC), Michael Callahan, Michael Harrington and Jeff Negalha (Assistants), Marco Genee (Dir of Ops). **Every one publishes a direct email** — unusual at this level and a real advantage for outreach.

**Mascot:** Rameses, a live ram. Colors Carolina blue and white.

### Academics — and the admissions problem

**Read this section before getting attached to the soccer.**

UNC is ranked **#22 nationally, #4 among public universities and #14 for best value**. 21,075 undergraduates on a 729-acre campus. Student-faculty ratio **14:1**, retention **97%**, six-year graduation **92%** — the best academic performance figures of any school in this research. Founded 1789, it is **the oldest public university in the United States.**

In-state tuition and fees $9,096; **out-of-state $45,228**. Average net price for loan recipients $12,414.

**Now the problem. North Carolina state law caps non-resident undergraduate enrolment at 18%, and the out-of-state acceptance rate runs around 8% — harder than Cornell.** The student body is roughly 82% in-state by statute, not preference. Among enrolled Class of 2028 students, **97% had a weighted GPA of 4.0 or above and 77% finished in the top tenth of their high school class.** A 4.0 weighted GPA is the floor, not the ceiling.

An international applicant sits in that non-resident pool. **Confirm directly with admissions how international students are counted against the 18% cap**, because it materially changes the odds.

**The one route that changes this maths is recruited athletics.** Admissions for recruited athletes runs through a different process with coaching-staff support, which is precisely why the soccer conversation matters more here than at any other school on the list. It is not a guarantee. Ask the staff directly and early how much admissions support they can provide for an international recruit — that single question determines whether UNC is realistic or aspirational.

**Testing:** UNC requires standardised tests through the 2025-26 cycle. From the Class of 2031 onward, applicants with a weighted GPA of 2.8 or higher will not be required to submit scores.

**Business — Kenan-Flagler.** Ranked **#8 nationally for undergraduate business** by Poets&Quants in 2026, up one place from 2025 — **the highest-ranked general business program on either list**, ahead of Northeastern's #20 and SMU's #25. Only South Carolina's International Business program outranks it in a specialism.

Also nationally ranked in journalism (Hussman School), public health and pharmacy, with a premier pre-med pipeline through UNC Health. **Honors Carolina** provides smaller classes and enhanced advising.

### Geography

**A genuine college town, and among the most attractive settings on either list.** Chapel Hill is a town of roughly 60,000 that exists around the university. Franklin Street runs along the campus edge as the commercial and social spine. The 729-acre campus is leafy, historic and walkable — the oldest parts date to the 1790s.

Chapel Hill, Durham and Raleigh form the Research Triangle, about 2 million people.

**Airports:** Raleigh-Durham International about 25 minutes, with direct Air Canada service to Toronto.

**Canadian border:** roughly 700 miles to Buffalo–Niagara, about **11 hours**.

**Nearby:** Durham 20 minutes, Raleigh 30, NC State across town, the Atlantic beaches 2.5 hours, the Blue Ridge 3.5.

**Housing:** first-year live-on is standard with a substantial residential system. Chapel Hill's off-campus market is more expensive than Raleigh's, reflecting the town's desirability and small size.

### Known for

Being the first public university in the United States. Basketball above all — the Dean Smith Center, Michael Jordan, six national titles. The women's soccer dynasty under Anson Dorrance, with 21 national championships, is the most dominant record in the history of American college athletics. Strong across journalism, public health, business and medicine.

**Bottom line:** the best combination of soccer pedigree and academic quality anywhere on either list, paired with the hardest admissions barrier. Worth pursuing precisely because the athletic route is the one that can open it — but have the admissions conversation with the coaching staff before investing heavily.

## UNC Charlotte — Charlotte, North Carolina

### Soccer program

**This is the best-value soccer program among the extras, and possibly on either list.** Charlotte's website still blocks automated access to the staff directory, but everything else came through.

**Charlotte are the reigning American Conference regular-season champions and were picked first in the 2026 preseason poll.** They also won back-to-back American tournament titles in 2023 and 2024, beating FIU in overtime in the 2024 final.

Founded 1976. **National runners-up in 2011** — the first team in any sport in school history to reach an NCAA national championship — with College Cup appearances in 1996 and 2011 and **17 NCAA tournament appearances**. The 1996 side was the first Conference USA team in any sport to reach a national semifinal, winning a school-record 19 games before losing to FIU in front of 20,269 in Richmond.

Manufor and Ogata were named preseason Players of the Year for 2026.

Early 2026 form places them around No. 18–25 nationally, having drawn 2-2 with then-No. 7 Vermont and lost 2-1 to No. 13 South Carolina — competitive against the best sides on this list.

**Staff:** Kevin Langan (HC). The athletics site blocks automated access to the staff directory, so assistant names and emails require a manual visit to charlotte49ers.com.

**Mascot:** Norm the Niner. Colors green and white.

### Academics

Public, founded 1946. **24,868 undergraduates** (31,000+ total) on a **1,000-acre campus**. US News **#143 nationally** and **#38 for Most Innovative Schools**. A **Carnegie R1** research institution — the only public R1 in North Carolina's largest city.

Student-faculty ratio **21:1** with an average undergraduate class size around 35 and 71% full-time faculty. Retention 85%, four-year graduation **50%**, median salary six years out **$48,812**.

**Cost is excellent.** In-state $7,020; **out-of-state $22,274** — third cheapest in this research, effectively level with Kennesaw State and behind only FIU. Average net price $15,018 with 52% receiving need-based aid.

Minimum GPA 2.5. **Test-optional applies to applicants with a GPA of 2.8 or higher**; below that, scores are required with a minimum SAT of 930 or ACT 17. Required high school coursework: four years English, four maths, three science, two of a foreign language, two social studies.

**Two things here are unusually well suited to a maths, CS or business student.**

First, **the College of Computing and Informatics runs the first School of Data Science in the Carolinas**, and Charlotte offers the **only BS in Sports Analytics in the Southeast**. For someone interested in both computing and sport, that combination does not exist anywhere else on either list.

Second, **Belk College of Business is the largest business school in North Carolina** with a record 5,140 undergraduates, ranked in the **top 25% of undergraduate business programs nationally**. Not Kenan-Flagler's #8, but substantial and tied directly into Charlotte's banking economy.

**Urban-adjacent with excellent transit.** The 1,000-acre campus sits in University City, about nine miles northeast of uptown Charlotte. **The LYNX Blue Line light rail runs directly from campus into the city centre** — Fortune 500 headquarters are a train ride away rather than a drive.

Charlotte is the second-largest banking centre in the United States, home to Bank of America's headquarters and major operations for Truist and Wells Fargo. Niche named this the **#1 most beautiful public campus** in the country.

**Airports:** Charlotte Douglas International is 25–30 minutes and is a major American Airlines hub with **direct service to Toronto**.

**Canadian border:** roughly 650 miles to Buffalo–Niagara, about **10 hours** — the shortest of any school in the Carolinas or Georgia.

**Nearby:** uptown Charlotte 20 minutes by rail, the Blue Ridge about 2 hours, Raleigh 2.5, the Atlantic coast 3.5.

**Housing:** substantial on-campus system with a large surrounding student rental market in University City, considerably cheaper than Chapel Hill.

### Known for

Rapid growth into North Carolina's urban research flagship. Computing, data science and engineering. Deep banking and finance connections through the Belk College. A campus regularly cited as among the most attractive in the country.

**Bottom line: on a soccer-plus-cost-plus-access basis this may be the strongest single option in the research.** A program that won the conference last season and is picked first this season, at roughly 80% admission, $22,274 out-of-state, ten hours from the border, with direct Toronto flights. The only gap is the blocked staff directory.

## Florida Atlantic — Boca Raton, Florida

### Soccer program

**2025 was the breakthrough season in 45 years of trying.** FAU won the American Conference tournament and made the **first NCAA tournament appearance in program history**, finishing 12-4-2 before losing 3-2 at UCF in the first round. They enter 2026 defending that conference crown and are currently ranked around **No. 19 nationally.**

That context matters, because the history before it was thin. **Joey Worthen has been head coach since February 2017**, so the breakthrough came in his ninth season — a slow build rather than a quick fix, which is usually the more durable kind.

**Staff:** Joey Worthen (HC), Lucas Champenois (Assistant). Emails not yet retrieved — still in the unswept column for contacts.

**Mascot:** Owlsley the Owl. Colors blue and red.

### Academics

Public, founded 1961 and opened 1964. **24,942 undergraduates**, 31,000+ total across six campuses along the southeast Florida coast, with the main **850-acre campus** in Boca Raton. Carnegie R1. The most ethnically and culturally diverse institution in Florida's State University System.

US News ranks FAU **#183 nationally** and **#30 for social mobility**. Student-faculty ratio **24:1**, among the highest here. Retention 81–84%, four-year graduation **50%**, six-year around 63%. Median salary six years out **$45,647**.

On flight convenience for an Ontario family, FAU is among the best-placed schools on either list.

**Nearby:** the beach 5 minutes, Fort Lauderdale 30, Miami 50, West Palm Beach 25.

### Known for

Rapid growth into an R1 research university. Engineering and computer science by volume. Ocean and coastal research. A recently ascendant athletics department — the men's basketball Final Four run in 2023 put the school on the national map, and the soccer breakthrough followed.

**Bottom line:** the cheapest realistic option in the research once net price is accounted for, attached to a soccer program that just won its conference for the first time and is ranked in the top 20. The caveats are a 1,000-seat stadium, a 24:1 ratio, and mixed reviews of the engineering college. Contacts still need gathering.

## Clemson — Clemson, South Carolina

### Soccer program

**Correction to earlier entries: Clemson has four national championships, not two.** 1984, 1987, 2021 and 2023, plus runner-up finishes in 1979 and 2015 and **ten College Cup semifinal appearances**. Thirty-eight NCAA tournament appearances. Founded 1934, this is **the oldest men's soccer program in this research at 92 years** and the most decorated.

**Mike Noonan is entering his 17th season** and is a two-time National Coach of the Year. He took over in 2010 and rebuilt the program — known nationally as "Clemson United" — back to the top.

**The 2023 championship season is worth understanding because of how it started.** The Tigers lost their opener at home to eventual No. 1 UCF, then lost at No. 14 Wake Forest on 22 September. That was their last defeat of the year: they finished **10-0-4 over the final 14 matches**, taking the ACC tournament crown, the national championship over Notre Dame, the ACC Tournament MVP, the NCAA Tournament MVP and the MAC Hermann Trophy.

**Consistency since is remarkable: Clemson has reached the ACC championship game in five of the past six seasons.** 2024 finished 15-3-4, ranked No. 7, with another ACC tournament title and a No. 9 national seed.

**2025 was a down year by record — 8-6-3 overall, 4-2-2 in the ACC — yet they still won the ACC tournament and earned a 38th NCAA appearance.** They opened ranked ninth, climbed to sixth, and beat South Carolina 4-1 in the Palmetto Series before fading. A mediocre record that still produced a conference championship tells you how high the floor is.

**The professional development record is the single strongest argument for Clemson, and it is current.** In 2025 the Tigers **led every NCAA program in the country with four first-round MLS SuperDraft selections**, and Kwaku Agyabeng became the **tenth Clemson player to sign a Generation adidas contract**. If the goal is professional soccer, no program on either list has a better recent conversion rate.

Home is **Historic Riggs Field, capacity 6,500** — one of the genuine home-field advantages in American college soccer, with **486 home wins across 60 years**. The 2019 side went 13-0-2 at Riggs and scored 48 goals there.

**2026:** eight ACC matches, opening against Furman on 20 August, with the Palmetto Series at South Carolina on 28 August — the fixture that drew South Carolina's record crowd of 7,671.

**Staff:** Mike Noonan (HC, 17th season), Philip Jones (Assoc HC, 14th season), Camilo Rodriguez (11th season), Gaye Diadie (2nd season). **Extraordinary staff continuity.** Emails not yet retrieved.

**Mascot:** The Tiger. Colors orange and regalia.

### Academics

Public land-grant, founded 1889. **23,401 undergraduates** (~28,000 total) across seven colleges. The campus is **17,000 acres — by far the largest in this research**, though most is forest and experimental farmland; the academic core is compact and walkable.

US News ranks Clemson **#75 nationally** and **#28 for Most Innovative Schools**. Student-faculty ratio **16:1**. **Six-year graduation is 86–87%, second only to UNC's 92% among everything profiled here**, with 54.6% of enrolled students having finished in the top tenth of their high school class.

**The earnings figure is the best in this research.** Median earnings four years after graduation are **$75,142** — and note that is at four years, while NJIT's $69,591 and NC State's $57,623 are measured at six.

**But the cost has risen sharply.** Out-of-state cost of attendance now **exceeds $60,000 annually**, and only 34% of first-year students receive need-based aid, with an average net price around $22,300. Clemson is no longer the cheap Southern public it once was — roughly three times FAU and nearly double NC State for an out-of-state student.

Admission has tightened correspondingly: recent acceptance rates run **38–49%** depending on cycle, clustering near 40%. Test-optional. Average SAT around 1325, middle-50% ACT 28–32.

**Business and engineering are top-20 public programs.** Largest majors by degrees conferred: management, marketing, financial management, biological sciences, psychology, mechanical engineering, computer science, nursing, accounting, economics.

**Computer science** is ranked **#83 nationally** by US News, with 40 full-time faculty in the School of Computing — comparable to FIU's #106 and behind UCSB's #35 or Ohio State's #38.

**Creative Inquiry** is the distinctive academic feature: undergraduate research teams working on real problems — biofuels, autonomous vehicles at the CU-ICAR automotive campus — from early in the degree. One caveat from student accounts: despite the 16:1 ratio, general-education lectures still run around 300 seats.

### Geography

**A genuine small college town in a rural setting, and the most scenic location in this research.** Clemson is a town of roughly 17,000 in the South Carolina Upstate, on Lake Hartwell at the foot of the Blue Ridge. Football Saturdays define the calendar. Closer to Penn State's model than anything urban.

**Airports are the weak point.** Greenville-Spartanburg International is about 45 minutes but regional, with no direct Toronto service. Charlotte Douglas is roughly 2 hours by car and Atlanta about the same — both offer direct Toronto flights, but that is a long transfer at either end. **One of the two or three weakest air links on either list.**

**Canadian border:** roughly 800 miles, about **12 to 13 hours**.

**Nearby:** Greenville 35 minutes, Atlanta 2 hours, Charlotte 2 hours, the Blue Ridge and Appalachian Trail immediately north.

**Housing:** first-year students are required to live on campus. The town market is small and demand outstrips supply, pushing some students into neighbouring communities.

### Known for

Football above all — Memorial Stadium, "Death Valley", two recent national championships — which sets the tone of the institution. Engineering, business and agricultural sciences. Creative Inquiry undergraduate research.

**Bottom line: the best professional soccer pathway in the research and the highest post-graduation earnings, paired with the highest out-of-state cost among the public options and a poor airport situation.** If the goal is professional soccer and the budget can absorb $60,000+, Clemson is the strongest case on either list. If cost or travel convenience matter, UNC Charlotte delivers most of the soccer at a third of the price.

## Appalachian State — REMOVED, no varsity men's soccer

**Appalachian State cut its men's soccer program on 26 May 2020 and has not reinstated it.** Every earlier reference to App State in this document as a Sun Belt men's soccer option is wrong and should be disregarded.

Athletic director Doug Gillin announced the discontinuation of three men's programs — soccer, tennis and indoor track and field — citing the financial impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. App State had been sponsoring the most sports in the Sun Belt and among the most in the Group of Five, and concluded it could not sustain 20 programs responsibly.

**The timing makes it especially frustrating.** The program was cut immediately after a strong 2019 season in which App State went 11-7-1 and finished 69th of 206 in the NCAA's RPI — the top third nationally. They had real history too: NCAA tournament appearances in 1975, 1977, 1978 and 1980 with Round of 16 runs in 1977 and 1978, a conference tournament title in 1990, and eleven conference regular-season championships between 1972 and 1989. Hank Steinbrecher, later executive director of US Soccer, once coached there.

**App State's decision also triggered a chain reaction.** It was the first domino that led the Sun Belt to discontinue men's soccer after 2020-21: Howard left for the Northeast Conference in July 2020, dropping the SBC to four men's soccer members — two short of the six required for an automatic NCAA berth. The Sun Belt only reinstated the sport in 2022.

**Soccer does still exist in Boone, just not NCAA soccer.** Former head coach Jason O'Keefe and businessman Michael Hitchcock founded **Appalachian FC** in the aftermath, now playing in **USL League Two** at the old ASU Soccer Stadium. A summer amateur league rather than a college pathway, but worth knowing about.

**Running tally of programs cut since 2020:** Cincinnati (April 2020) and Appalachian State (May 2020) — both originally on these lists, both gone.

### The vacancy this creates

Removing App State drops the extras from 14 schools to 13. The natural replacement in the same size band is **Duke** (~18,000, ACC, Durham NC) — a real program in the strongest conference in college soccer, sitting in the same enrollment range as UNC Wilmington and UNC Greensboro. Worth adding to hold the list at 14.

## Nova Southeastern — Davie / Fort Lauderdale, Florida

### The size caveat, now confirmed

I flagged earlier that NSU's ~21,000 enrolment was mostly graduate and professional students. That is now confirmed: **undergraduate enrolment is 7,311.** Total enrolment is around 20,900, meaning roughly **13,500 of NSU's students are in graduate or professional programs** — medicine, dental, pharmacy, optometry, law and psychology.

**In practical terms NSU is a small undergraduate university**, almost exactly the same undergraduate size as SMU (7,285). It only clears the 18,000 threshold on a technicality. If the size criterion was a proxy for a large undergraduate campus, **NSU does not meet it.**

### Soccer program

NCAA **Division II**, Sunshine State Conference — one of the strongest D2 leagues in the country.

**Matt McArthur** was promoted to head coach in January 2025 and enters his third season. He was an NSU assistant in 2023 and began coaching at the University of Mobile in 2022, having played central midfield there — a two-time SSAC Second-Team All-Conference selection. **He is a young coach at an early-career stage**, which cuts both ways: energy and accessibility against limited track record.

**Results are steady rather than spectacular.** 2025 finished 8-4-3 overall and 5-3-2 in conference, opening with a **2-0 upset of No. 5 Clayton State**, then entering the SSC tournament as the fifth seed and losing 2-1 at Rollins. 2024 was 7-5-3 with a **6-0-1 home record**, exiting on penalties to Tampa.

McArthur has developed players: **Santiago Hoyos Lopez** earned First Team All-SSC and All-South Region Second Team in 2025, ranking **sixth nationally in points per game**, with Liam Healey and Mani Steinbjornsson taking Third Team honours.

**2026: picked fifth in the SSC preseason coaches' poll.** Fourteen regular-season matches with eight at home, opening with three consecutive home fixtures. Rivalries with Barry and Lynn.

**The roster composition is the most relevant fact here.** The returning core is Henrik Dahl, Sebastian Valdez, Matthew Gordon, Andres Morral Rosales, Markus Rokseth, Santino Scerbo, Jamie Bulloch, Aidan Marshall King, Toga Katsuma and Nikolas Ricardo — Scandinavian, Spanish, Japanese, British and Latin American names throughout. **This is a heavily international squad**, which tells you the staff recruits abroad as a matter of course. For a Canadian player that is a materially better signal than Georgia Southern's 95%-in-state student body.

**Money works differently at D2.** Athletic scholarships are available but on an equivalency basis — partial awards split across a roster rather than full rides — and can be stacked with need-based and academic aid.

NSU's athletic department has real resources: **men's basketball won D2 national championships in 2023 and 2025** with four straight Final Fours, and baseball won the 2016 D2 College World Series.

**Staff:** Matt McArthur (HC). Assistant names and emails not yet retrieved.

**Mascot:** Razor the Shark. Colors navy blue and gray.

### Academics

Private, founded 1964 as Nova University of Advanced Technology, merged with Southeastern University in 1971. **7,311 undergraduates** on a **314-acre campus**. A Hispanic-serving institution with 18 colleges offering 50 undergraduate majors.

US News ranks NSU **#257 nationally** and #204 for social mobility. **Student-faculty ratio 16:1** — better than every large public on this list, the genuine upside of a small undergraduate population. Four-year graduation **55%**, median salary six years out **$58,153**.

Admission is accessible at **76%**. Tuition and fees are **$40,200**, with 65% of first-years receiving need-based aid and an average net price of **$28,219**. Note the extras: roughly $11,200 for room and board, a $600 per semester student services fee, and mandatory health insurance around $2,092 a year if not otherwise covered. **Undergraduate enrolment is 28% male and 72% female** — the most skewed ratio on either list.

**The genuine academic strength is marine science.** The Halmos College of Natural Sciences and Oceanography runs coral reef restoration work and NSU offers scuba instruction at its on-campus aquatics facility.

**For the stated subjects, I would be cautious.** A US News reviewer states plainly that NSU places overwhelming institutional emphasis on the medical school and health-related fields, that other schools are held back as a result, and advises against enrolling to study business. That is one voice rather than data — but it is consistent with the structure of an institution where two-thirds of students are in graduate and professional health programs. The law school is ranked in the bottom tier nationally (178-196). **If business, computer science, maths or physics is the target, this is the weakest academic fit among the extras.**

### Geography

**Suburban Davie, in the Fort Lauderdale metro.** The 314-acre campus is compact and modern but car-dependent. Residence halls exist only at the Fort Lauderdale/Davie campus.

**Airports are excellent.** Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International is about 20 minutes and carries **heavy direct Toronto and Montreal traffic**; Miami International is roughly 40 minutes.

**Canadian border:** roughly 1,450 miles, about **22 hours**. Fly.

**Nearby:** the Atlantic beaches about 20 minutes, downtown Fort Lauderdale 20, Miami 40, the Everglades immediately west.

### Known for

Health professions above all. Marine science and oceanography. A dominant recent Division II basketball program. A pragmatic, professional, career-focused culture rather than a traditional undergraduate one.

**Bottom line: the soccer fit is decent and the international roster is a real positive, but the institution is a graduate-and-health-professions university with a small undergraduate college attached.** At $40,200 tuition with a $28,219 net price, it costs more than FAU, FIU, UNC Charlotte or NC State while ranking below all of them. Last among the extras unless marine science is of interest.

## Keiser — West Palm Beach, Florida

### The size problem, and it is worse than Nova Southeastern's

**The flagship residential campus in West Palm Beach has approximately 1,500 students.**

The ~20,000 figure that put Keiser on this list is the entire Keiser system: **21 Florida campuses plus online delivery and locations in China and Nicaragua**, overwhelmingly commuter, career-programme and online students. The satellite campuses run heavily to associate degrees and certificates — massage therapy, medical assisting, surgical technology, pharmacy technician.

The place a soccer recruit would actually live and study is a **100-acre residential campus with about 1,500 students**, built in 2015 on the former Northwood University site. The Common Application lists it in the "2,000 and under" size band.

**Keiser does not meet the 18,000 criterion in any meaningful sense.** That said, the soccer is genuinely good, so it is worth knowing about on its own merits.

### Soccer program

**NAIA, Sun Conference — and this is a strong program.**

**2025 was outstanding: 16-1-2, winning both the Sun Conference regular season and tournament titles**, the latter 2-1 in overtime over St. Thomas. They finished ranked **No. 16 nationally**, went **11-1 at home** and **7-0-1 in conference play**, and reached the second round of the NAIA National Tournament before losing 2-1 to Warner Pacific.

That was the **fifth Sun Conference regular-season title** (2019, 2020, 2022, 2024, 2025), and Keiser has reached the **NAIA opening rounds in seven consecutive seasons**.

**Correction to an earlier note in this document:** I previously recorded Keiser as 2021 NAIA national champions. I cannot verify that from the program's own materials, which describe the recent peak as the 2025 conference double and a second-round national tournament exit. **Treat the national championship claim as unverified.** (Keiser's *football* programme did win the NAIA national title in 2023.)

**Gavin Oldham enters his 10th season**, and **the coaching staff was named NAIA South Staff of the Year** after last season. Joe Barber joins for 2026 as the new assistant head coach. Santiago Nebot, the former goalkeeper, has returned as a graduate assistant after back-to-back Sun Conference Golden Gloves and United Soccer Coaches All-American honours.

**2026: opened ranked No. 15**, returning seven All-Sun Conference selections across roughly 15–17 matches with eight or nine at **Arrigo–Vecellio Field**.

**The academic-athletic combination is genuinely strong**, unusual at this level. Boomkens earned NAIA Third-Team All-American and United Soccer Coaches All-American honours *and* CSC Academic All-American; Duque was also a CSC Academic All-American; Bolland took CSC All-District.

**NAIA scholarship rules are more flexible than NCAA rules** — athletic aid can be combined with academic and need-based aid with fewer restrictions, and eligibility standards are less rigid than the NCAA's 2.3 core-GPA sliding scale. For a player whose academic profile makes NCAA eligibility tight, the NAIA route is a real option.

### Academics — read this carefully

Founded 1977 by Arthur Keiser and his mother Evelyn as **a career college** in Fort Lauderdale. Converted from for-profit to not-for-profit in 2011. Accredited by SACSCOC.

**Admission is effectively open: 96–97% acceptance.** No application fee, no essay, no letter of recommendation required, test-optional, rolling deadlines. US News has placed Keiser in the **top 25 for social mobility** four years running.

Tuition runs roughly **$34,000–$39,700** with room and board around **$15,000** — total cost broadly comparable to Nova Southeastern and well above FAU, FIU or UNC Charlotte. Graduation rate 55%.

**The subject fit is the problem, and it is serious.** Keiser's catalogue centres on nursing, allied health, social and behavioural sciences, criminal justice and business administration. The Flagship campus lists **29 majors**. This is a career-focused institution by design and history — not a research university — and **mathematics and physics are very unlikely to exist as majors.** Computing exists mainly as information-technology programmes rather than a research-oriented computer science degree.

**Of the four stated academic criteria — maths, physics, computer science, business — Keiser meets one.**

### Geography

**West Palm Beach**, on a 100-acre campus off Okeechobee Boulevard near Florida's Turnpike. Genuinely residential with modern athletic facilities, built deliberately to offer a traditional campus experience.

**Airports:** Palm Beach International about 15 minutes; Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood roughly 45 minutes with heavy Toronto and Montreal service.

**Canadian border:** roughly 1,400 miles, about **21 hours**. Fly.

**Nearby:** the Atlantic beaches 15 minutes, Fort Lauderdale 45, Miami 70, Orlando about 2.5 hours.

### Known for

Florida's largest private non-profit university by system enrolment. Career-focused education in health and business. Social mobility outcomes. A recently dominant NAIA athletics department — football won the 2023 NAIA national championship and has reached the title game four years running.

**Bottom line: excellent soccer, and NAIA eligibility rules are a genuinely useful fallback if NCAA academic eligibility is ever in question.** But a 1,500-student career-focused campus at roughly $50,000 all-in, without maths or physics, is a long way from what the rest of this list offers.

## UNC Wilmington — Wilmington, North Carolina

### Soccer program

**Aidan Heaney enters his 26th season at UNCW and his 28th as a Division I head coach** — the second-longest tenure in this entire research, behind only Tim Vom Steeg at UCSB. Since taking over in 2001 he has produced **208 wins, 18 CAA tournament berths and four NCAA tournament appearances**, along with the program's only CAA tournament championship (2009) and four regular-season titles.

**The single most useful fact about this program for a recruit: Heaney plays young players, and there is hard evidence for it.**

In 2024 he took a roster containing **20 combined freshmen and sophomores** to the CAA Championship Game, the program's first final since 2019. The goals in that run came from **first-year players** — Christian Acevedo, Cesare D'Amico and Osei Gyamfi. A **redshirt freshman goalkeeper**, Trey Smiley, kept a clean sheet in the conference semifinal, and a **redshirt sophomore**, Bennett Jones, saved three penalties in the shootout that beat Monmouth in the first round. Gyamfi made TopDrawerSoccer's Postseason Freshmen Top 100 at No. 74 and the CAA All-Rookie Team with two goals and five assists across 19 games.

No other program in this document demonstrates that pattern as clearly. If early minutes matter more than the badge, this is the strongest evidence anywhere on either list.

**They also punch up.** The 2023 side went 6-6-4 but won on the road at South Carolina and drew with a nationally ranked Clemson. This season they beat Georgia Southern 2-0 on 24 August.

### Academics

Public, founded 1947. **15,254 undergraduates** (roughly 18,800 total) on a **661-acre campus**. Carnegie R2.

**Another rankings mismatch, of the same kind as Georgia State's.** US News places UNCW **#198 nationally** but **#58 for Best Undergraduate Teaching**. Student-faculty ratio is **17:1** with an **average class size around 25**, and Princeton Review student commentary consistently cites small classes and accessible faculty.

Admission runs roughly **62–68%** depending on the source. Median SAT 1280, ACT 27. Test-optional. Four-year graduation **56%**, and **92% of graduates are employed or in graduate school within six months**, though median salary six years out is a modest **$46,972**.

**A cost flag that matters more here than anywhere else so far.** In-state tuition and fees are $7,266 and **out-of-state $25,420** — but reported **international undergraduate tuition is around $42,603**, substantially above the out-of-state rate. Most schools in this document charge international students the out-of-state figure. **Verify UNCW's international rate directly before treating the $25,420 number as applicable** — the gap is roughly $17,000 a year.

**Marine biology is the institutional crown jewel** — regularly described as among the best programs in the world, supported by the waterfront Center for Marine Science where undergraduates do fieldwork alongside faculty, and a full PhD in Marine Biology. Film studies benefits from Wilmington's "Hollywood East" production industry, and creative writing has a strong literary reputation.

**For the stated subjects:** the Cameron School of Business is solid with concentrations in finance and marketing, and computer science exists within Arts and Sciences. But this is not a STEM-forward institution the way NJIT, NC State or UCSB are. The most popular majors are nursing, business, psychology, biology and kinesiology. **Maths and physics exist but are small departments.**

### Geography

**A coastal campus, and the beach proximity genuinely defines it.** Wilmington sits between the Cape Fear River and the Atlantic, with **Wrightsville Beach about ten minutes from campus** and the historic downtown Riverwalk roughly the same. Wilmington is a city of about 120,000 with a real independent identity rather than a pure college town.

**All first-year students are required to live on campus.** UNCW houses roughly 4,000 students across 35 residence halls at about $8,000 a year, with a large new residence hall opening in 2026 adding over 500 beds.

**Airports are the weak point.** Wilmington International is 15 minutes away but small, routing through Charlotte or Atlanta. Raleigh-Durham is about 2 hours by car and Charlotte 3.5. **Comparable to Clemson and Georgia Southern — among the weakest links in this research.**

**Canadian border:** roughly 750 miles to Buffalo–Niagara, about **12 hours**.

**Nearby:** Wrightsville Beach 10 minutes, Raleigh 2 hours, Charlotte 3.5, Myrtle Beach 75 minutes.

### Known for

Marine biology and coastal research above all. Film studies, supported by a genuine local production industry. A coastal lifestyle that defines student life. Undergraduate teaching quality that substantially exceeds the institution's overall ranking.

**Bottom line: the clearest evidence on either list that freshmen get real minutes, under a coach with 26 years of stability, at a school that teaches undergraduates well.** The reservations are a weak airport, modest earnings outcomes, a thin STEM profile, and an international tuition rate that needs checking before anything else.

## UNC Greensboro — Greensboro, North Carolina

### Soccer program

**Ranked last on a size-sorted list, this is one of the four or five best soccer programs in the entire research.** That mismatch is the most important thing in this profile.

**Five NCAA national championships — 1982, 1983, 1985, 1986 and 1987 at Division III** — plus a Division II runner-up finish in 1989. Founded 1971, UNCG holds **23 conference regular-season championships and 15 conference tournament titles**, and has made **13 Division I NCAA tournament appearances** since moving up.

Nine players made All-SoCon teams.

**Chris Rich** was appointed on 30 July 2019 and has delivered **four SoCon regular-season titles, two SoCon tournament championships and three NCAA tournament appearances** in seven seasons. An earlier side under him finished **13-4-1 (.750)**, the best winning percentage in program history. He has coached three All-Americans — Theo Collomb, JC Ngando and Dovlo — plus an Academic All-American in Marco Milanese, and goalkeeper Niclas Wild won back-to-back SoCon Goalkeeper of the Year awards.

### Academics

Public, founded 1891 as a women's college, renamed in 1963 upon admitting men. **14,416 undergraduates** (roughly 18,700 total) on a **266-acre urban campus**. Carnegie R2, with 175 undergraduate degrees across six colleges and 28 residence halls.

**The value case is strong and the outcomes case is weak. Both are true.**

On value: US News ranks UNCG **#198 nationally but #13 in the country for social mobility**, and Washington Monthly placed it **12th for Best Bang for the Buck in the Southeast**. In-state tuition is $7,662 and **out-of-state $24,014**, with 71% of first-years receiving need-based aid and an **average net price of $10,907 — the second lowest in this entire research, behind only FAU's $8,636.** Student-faculty ratio 17–18:1.

Eleven undergraduate programs including accounting, business analytics, finance, international business, marketing, management information systems and supply chain management.

**Urban and central.** Greensboro is a city of about 300,000 and the anchor of the Piedmont Triad, a metro of roughly 1.7 million alongside Winston-Salem and High Point. The 266-acre campus sits within the city.

The position is genuinely convenient — **Greensboro sits between Charlotte and Raleigh**, roughly 90 minutes from one and 75 from the other, putting UNC, NC State, Duke, Wake Forest and UNC Charlotte all within easy reach.

**Airports:** Piedmont Triad International is about 15 minutes from campus — modest, but routing through Charlotte or Atlanta is straightforward. Charlotte Douglas, with direct Toronto service, is about 90 minutes by car, and Raleigh-Durham 75 minutes. **Better than Wilmington, Clemson or Georgia Southern.**

**Canadian border:** roughly 700 miles to Buffalo–Niagara, about **11 hours** — among the closest in the Carolinas.

**Nearby:** Winston-Salem 30 minutes, Raleigh 75, Charlotte 90, the Blue Ridge about 2 hours.

### Known for

Origins as a women's college and a continuing emphasis on access. Education, nursing and the performing arts. Social mobility outcomes among the best in the country. And, quietly, one of the most successful men's soccer programs in the American South.

**Bottom line: the biggest mismatch in this research between soccer quality and list position.** A Sweet Sixteen team that beat No. 2 Virginia, produced two first-round MLS picks in a single draft, and holds five national championships — at 89% admission, $24,014 out-of-state, a $10,907 average net price and 11 hours from the border. The counterweights are a 39% four-year graduation rate and the second-lowest earnings figure here. **If soccer level per dollar is the criterion, this belongs near the top of the extras, not at the bottom.**

# Canadian schools — profiles

## University of Toronto — Toronto, Ontario

### Soccer program

**Founded in 1879 — 147 years ago. By far the oldest program in the entire research**, predating Clemson's 1934 founding by more than half a century.

**The Varsity Blues hold 51 OUA provincial championships, an all-time conference record**, plus one U SPORTS national title in 1988 and seventeen national championship appearances.

**2025 produced something almost unprecedented.** The men won their 51st OUA championship with a **2-0 win over York at Varsity Stadium**, and the women won their first-ever title the same year. **That joint sweep has happened only three times before in OUA history** — Western in 1998 and 2004, Laurier in 2008. The men led the OUA with 42 goals and conceded a province-low eight.

**Ilya Orlov's background is the most internationally developed of any coach in this document.** Appointed head coach in January 2019 after four years as lead assistant, he holds a career record of **45-15-12**. He is simultaneously head coach of **Alliance United FC in League1 Ontario**, and previously served as League1 assistant and under-21 head coach at North Toronto SC. His coaching education includes a stint as youth performance centre coach at **FC Union Berlin**, plus international internships at **Fulham FC, Feyenoord Rotterdam, Korona Kielce in Poland and KuPS in Finland.**

For a recruit, the League1 Ontario connection matters practically: Orlov is embedded in the same Ontario semi-professional ecosystem a local player would already be moving through.

**2026 is a rebuild and the early results show it.** Toronto lost nine starters from the championship side. Through four matches they drew McGill 1-1, **lost 1-2 at home to rivals TMU**, drew Queen's 1-1 and beat Nipissing 2-1 — roughly 1-1-2.

**Orlov has said explicitly that he is most excited to work with the younger players and see what the new faces contribute.** Given nine departures, that is a meaningful signal about minutes.

**Squad:** key returners are **Niklas Hallam** (midfield, last season's leading scorer with nine goals and four assists in twelve games) and **Toby Richardson** (defence). Key additions are forward **Michael Maslanka** and goalkeeper **Shane Perera**, with forward Avaneish Nandan and defender Tom Rosenberg in the recruiting class. Filip Zendelek kept goal last season with nine wins and 39 saves in eleven appearances.

**Season structure:** OUA play-in 21 October, quarter-final 24 October, semifinal 31 October, OUA Championship 7 November, then the **U SPORTS Championship from 12 to 15 November at the University of Victoria.**

Home is **Varsity Stadium**, capacity 4,000, on the downtown St. George campus. The inner-city rivalry with **TMU** is the fixture of the season.

### Academics

**The largest university in Canada and consistently its highest-ranked**, with roughly 98,000 students across three campuses — St. George downtown, Mississauga and Scarborough. Varsity athletics operate out of St. George.

**The single most important structural fact for a recruit is the Program of Study system.** At U of T you are generally admitted to a faculty or admission stream rather than directly to a major. After first year you apply to your specific program, and **Computer Science and Rotman Commerce are both limited-enrolment programs requiring strong first-year marks in specified courses.** This is the same trap as the restricted CS majors at Penn State, Ohio State, UCSB and NJIT, with one difference: at U of T the competition happens *after* you have already enrolled and started playing.

**That interacts badly with a first-year varsity season if the marks slip.** It is the single question worth putting to Orlov and to an academic advisor before committing: what happens to program admission if first-year grades dip during the soccer season, and what academic support exists for varsity athletes.

U of T also carries a well-known reputation for large first-year classes and demanding grading. Mathematics and physics are genuinely world-class — the Fields Institute is here — and computer science is among the strongest in Canada.

**The financial picture is the clearest advantage of the Canadian options.** As an Ontario domestic student the tuition is a fraction of any American out-of-state or international rate, even before considering that U SPORTS Athletic Financial Awards are capped at tuition and compulsory fees. Against Northeastern at $69,289 or UNCW's reported international rate near $42,603, the gap is enormous. **Verify current domestic rates for the specific faculty**, since Rotman Commerce and Engineering carry higher fees than Arts and Science.

### Geography

**Downtown, and the most urban campus in this entire research.** St. George occupies a large swathe of central Toronto, walkable throughout, with the subway running through it. Varsity Stadium sits on campus.

**Travel is the trivial part.** Toronto Pearson is roughly 30 minutes; Billy Bishop is downtown. Home matches require no flights at all — and away fixtures at Queen's, Trent, Nipissing, Ontario Tech, TMU and York are all within a few hours' drive.

### Known for

Canada's flagship research university — insulin, stem cells, and a research output unmatched nationally. Rotman Commerce, Engineering Science, the Fields Institute, and one of the strongest computer science faculties in the world. Enormous scale, and correspondingly less hand-holding than a smaller school.

**Bottom line: the strongest academic institution available to a Canadian student at domestic prices, attached to the winningest program in OUA history, entering a rebuild year with nine starters gone and a coach openly looking to younger players.** The reservation is the Program of Study system, which puts the real academic hurdle in first year rather than at admission.

**Bottom line: the best men's soccer programme in Canada, currently holding the national championship, with a roster built from MLS Next Pro and League1 Ontario, a stadium shared with a Canadian Premier League club, a coach who played ACC soccer at Maryland, and every senior coach reachable by email.** If the Canadian route is live at all, this is the first call.

## Western University — London, Ontario

### Soccer program

**Martin Painter is in his tenth season at Western and is one of the most decorated coaches in this document — but he coaches two teams.**

He has led the **women's** programme since January 2012, producing five OUA West Division titles, nine consecutive playoff appearances, seven OUA Final Four appearances, OUA silver medals in 2013, 2014, 2017, 2018 and 2021, a bronze in 2016, and **five U SPORTS National Championship appearances.** He was **U SPORTS Coach of the Year in 2013** and OUA West Coach of the Year in 2013 and 2017.

**He took on the men's programme in 2018 and won an OUA bronze medal that first season.**

**This dual role is the single most important thing to understand about Western.** Painter runs both squads simultaneously. At every other school in this research, the men's programme has a dedicated head coach. Ask directly how his time is divided, and who runs men's training day to day.

**His own background is excellent.** He **played four seasons for Western's men's team, winning three OUA championships and the 1999 U SPORTS National Championship** — an alumnus and a national champion as a player. He holds a **Canada Soccer A License** and three Western degrees: History (1998), Law (2001) and Education (2007).

He also coached **FC London from its inception in 2009 through 2015 — seven seasons in the Premier Development League, winning a North American championship in 2012** — and spent five seasons as head coach of Fanshawe College's women's programme, taking OCAA Coach of the Year in 2011.

**Program history:** Western won OUA championships in 1998 and 2004 and the national title in 1999. **Western is one of only three programmes ever to sweep both the men's and women's OUA titles in the same year**, doing it in 1998 and again in 2004 — the feat Toronto matched in 2025.

**2026:** Western opened away at York on 30 August, the only meeting between the two this season.

**Staff:** Martin Painter (Head Coach, Men's and Women's Soccer) — **mpainter@uwo.ca**. Assistant staff not yet retrieved; Painter's published bio was last updated in 2022, so verify the current structure directly.

**Mascot:** JW the Mustang. Colors purple and white.

### Academics

Roughly **40,000 students** in London, Ontario, a city of about 425,000 roughly two hours from Toronto. Western consistently ranks among Canada's top research universities and is particularly known for **the strongest undergraduate student experience of the Ontario schools** — a genuinely residential, traditional campus culture that neither U of T nor York offers.

**The Ivey Business School is the draw and the complication.** Ivey is an **Advanced Entry Opportunity**: you apply to another faculty first, complete two years, and enter Ivey for third and fourth year. The **AEO designation is granted at high-school admission** and carries a high entering average, and holding AEO status does not guarantee entry — you must maintain strong marks through first and second year.

**For an athlete this is the same structural risk as U of T's Program of Study system, spread across two years instead of one.**

Outside Ivey, Western is strong in medicine (Schulich School of Medicine and Dentistry), engineering, law and the social sciences. Mathematics, physics and computer science are offered through the Faculty of Science.

### Geography

**The most traditional campus environment of the original three Canadian schools.** Western's campus in north London is green, residential and self-contained, with Gothic Revival stone architecture — much closer in feel to Penn State or Clemson than to U of T's downtown sprawl or York's suburban layout.

London sits **roughly two hours from Toronto and under two hours from the Detroit–Windsor border crossing**, and is served by London International Airport for regional connections, with Pearson about two hours away by car.

### Known for

Ivey Business School. The strongest undergraduate campus culture in Ontario, with heavy residence participation and a well-known Homecoming tradition. Medicine and engineering. A soccer programme with a national championship in its history and a coach who played on that team.

**Bottom line: the best traditional campus experience of the three original Canadian options, with a genuinely decorated coach — but he runs two programmes at once, and Ivey's two-year AEO pathway puts the academic hurdle after enrolment rather than at admission.** Thin on published assistant staff and on recent men's-programme results compared with York and Toronto.

## University of Waterloo — Waterloo, Ontario

### Soccer program

**The academic headline school of the OUA West additions, with a program on the way back up.** Waterloo plays in OUA West, U SPORTS.

**The signature achievement is the 2001 OUA championship — a title 44 years in the making.** Under first-year head coach Peter Mackie the Warriors went 7-3-0, earned the program's first-ever national ranking, and won the gold-medal match **2-1 over Wilfrid Laurier** on two Nicholas Klassen free kicks, then finished fifth at the CIS national championship in Halifax. Six players made the OUA all-star teams and Mackie was named OUA Coach of the Year. That team is in the Warriors Hall of Fame.

**2025 was a genuine rebound.** Waterloo reached the OUA semifinal before losing 2-0 to No. 3 Toronto — a strong second-place OUA West finish after a lean 2024, in **Jon Morgan's** second season. Morgan was promoted from assistant to head coach in March 2024, having been on staff since 2017; he has an English semi-professional playing background and prior roles at Cambridge United and Conestoga College. Exact 2024–25 W-L-T records could not be verified against OUA's access-blocked standings pages, so confirm them directly.

Home matches are at **Warrior Field** on the main campus — a grandstand of roughly 1,700 (about 5,700 all-in) shared with the football program.

**Staff:** Jon Morgan (Head Coach) · Andrew Agyare-Tabbi (Assistant Coach and **Recruitment Manager**) · Sandro Palermo and Kevin Espiro (Assistant Coaches) · Michael Neptune (Goalkeeper Coach). **No coach emails are published** — recruiting is routed through the "Become a Warrior" form at athletics.uwaterloo.ca/sb_output.aspx?form=4, and Agyare-Tabbi is the intended recruiting target.

**Mascot:** King Warrior. Colors black and gold.

### Academics

**The strongest computer-science and engineering environment in this entire document.** Waterloo is home to the **first Faculty of Mathematics in North America (founded 1967)**, which houses the David R. Cheriton School of Computer Science.

**It also runs the largest co-operative education program in the world** — more than 20,000 undergraduate co-op students and 8,000+ employers across 70+ countries. CS and engineering students graduate with roughly two years of paid, alternating work experience. A Google engineering director has publicly named Waterloo one of Google's top three or four recruiting universities worldwide, alongside MIT and Carnegie Mellon.

Waterloo ranks No. 1 for reputation among Canada's comprehensive universities (Maclean's 2026). Enrollment is roughly 42,000. As with the other Canadian schools, admit rate and student-faculty ratio are not published in a comparable form — treat both as unavailable.

**For a recruit targeting computer science or engineering, this is the clearest academic pick of the six Canadian schools**, and unlike U of T's Program of Study or Western's AEO pathway, admission to Math/CS is a direct application decision worth confirming with admissions for the specific plan.

### Geography

**Waterloo is the twin city of Kitchener** — the Kitchener–Waterloo (KW) region, Canada's leading technology hub, nicknamed the "Silicon Valley of the North." BlackBerry's former campus (partly acquired by the university) and a significant Google engineering office are minutes away, with a dense startup scene feeding the co-op program.

The campus is large (~400 hectares). Toronto Pearson is roughly 90 minutes east, and the **Detroit–Windsor border crossing is about three hours** southwest via Highway 401. All OUA away fixtures are drivable; home matches require no flights.

### Known for

The world's biggest co-op program, quantum computing, and one of the strongest computer-science faculties anywhere. King Warrior; black and gold.

**Bottom line: the academic standout of the Canadian group for a computer-science or engineering athlete, with a program trending upward under Jon Morgan — the one catch is that no coach emails are published, so the recruit form is the entry point.**

## University of Guelph — Guelph, Ontario

### Soccer program

**The most professionally decorated coach of the Canadian additions now runs the program.** Guelph plays in OUA West, U SPORTS.

**Alan Koch was hired in January 2025** after longtime coach Keith Mason's retirement. He is a former head coach of **FC Cincinnati in MLS (2017–19)**, and previously coached FC Edmonton in the Canadian Premier League and the Colorado Springs Switchbacks in the USL. At Whitecaps FC 2 he gave **Alphonso Davies his professional debut.** At Simon Fraser (2008–15) he went 126-21-7 and reached two NCAA Division II Final Fours, and he was 2018 USL Coach of the Year. A native of Durban, South Africa.

**The Gryphons hold two OUA championships**, in 1990 and 2016 — the 2016 title, a 3-1 win over York, was the program's first in 26 years. They reached the **1990 CIAU national final**, losing 2-1 to defending champion UBC before a record home crowd. In 2024 they went 5-3-4 and won an opening-round OUA playoff on penalties over Laurier. Exact recent records should be confirmed against OUA's access-blocked standings pages.

Home matches are at the **Gryphon Soccer Complex** (FIFA 2-star certified turf), with some played at Alumni Stadium.

**The best animal- and life-sciences school in the country.** The **Ontario Veterinary College is ranked No. 1 in Canada and 6th in the world for veterinary science (QS 2025).** The Ontario Agricultural College sits around 2nd in Canada for agriculture, and the university is top-150 globally for life sciences.

Guelph is a top-6 comprehensive university and No. 1 for research dollars per Maclean's 2025. Enrollment is roughly 30,000 (about 25,000 undergraduate), drawn from 140+ countries. Admit rate and a reliable student-faculty ratio are not published in a comparable form — treat both as unavailable.

### Known for

Veterinary medicine and agriculture — the Ontario Veterinary and Agricultural Colleges — and one of Canada's most residential comprehensive campuses. Gryphon; red, black and gold.

## Wilfrid Laurier University — Waterloo, Ontario

### Soccer program

**A former national champion, next door to Waterloo.** Laurier plays in OUA West, U SPORTS. The formal name is Wilfrid Laurier University; the main campus is in Waterloo.

**The program's peak was back-to-back national titles in 2000 and 2001**, plus a 1980 CIAU national-final appearance. **OUA titles came in 2000 and 2008**, and in 2008 Laurier swept both the men's and women's OUA soccer championships in the same season — a rare double, and one of only a handful of such sweeps in OUA history.

**Mario Halapir** is the long-tenured head coach and was the **2008 OUA Coach of the Year.** Unlike Western, this is a dedicated men's role. Recent seasons have been mid-table in a deep OUA West — competitive but no longer a title favourite — and exact recent records should be confirmed against OUA's access-blocked standings pages.

Home matches are at **Ansley Alumni Field** on the Waterloo campus (football uses the separate University Stadium; a distinct soccer-field capacity is not published).

**Staff:** Mario Halapir (Head Coach) — **mariohalapir@icloud.com** · Luke Holder, James Rohrer and Santiago Almada (Assistant Coaches) · Nicole Smart (Assistant Coach, Strength & Conditioning). **Halapir's email is published and he is the direct first contact**; the assistants' addresses are not, and no public recruit form was found.

**Mascot:** Golden Hawks; Midas the Golden Hawk. Colors purple and gold.

### Academics

**The Lazaridis School of Business & Economics is the flagship** — Laurier's largest faculty and a nationally strong business and economics school, with graduate programs appearing in the QS global rankings. **The Faculty of Music is among Canada's best**, named "Canada's Best Music Campus" by CBC Radio and home to the Penderecki String Quartet. Laurier is also strong in psychology and social work.

Enrollment is roughly 23,000 across the Waterloo, Brantford and Milton campuses, with Waterloo the main site — a smaller, more undergraduate-focused school than Waterloo next door. Admit rate and a comparable student-faculty ratio are not published — treat both as unavailable.

### Geography

**The main campus is in Waterloo, essentially bordering the University of Waterloo** — the same KW technology region, but a more compact and social campus. Twin city with Kitchener, with the same startup and co-op ecosystem on the doorstep.

The **Detroit–Windsor border crossing is about three hours** southwest; Toronto Pearson is roughly 90 minutes east. All OUA away fixtures are drivable.

### Known for

The Lazaridis business school and a top-ranked music faculty, on a compact campus with a strong student culture. Golden Hawks; Midas; purple and gold.

**Bottom line: a former back-to-back national champion with a dedicated, decorated head coach whose email is published, at a school with a strong business program — sharing the KW tech region with Waterloo, though its recent results trail the top of OUA West.**
